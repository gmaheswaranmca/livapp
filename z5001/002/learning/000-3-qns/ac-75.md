AtCoder is widely considered the gold standard for competitive programming because its problems often focus on **mathematical insight, construction, and clever observation** rather than just implementing standard data structures.

To cover all essential solution patterns, you should focus on the **"Educational DP Contest (EDPC)"** for your DP foundation and the **"Typical 90"** contest for a broad spectrum of algorithmic patterns.

### Why this approach works:

1. **The "Educational DP Contest" (EDPC):** This is a mandatory rite of passage. It starts with simple "Frog" problems (1D DP) and scales up to complex bitmasking and tree DP. Solving all 26 problems here will give you more pattern-recognition power than any other single resource.
2. **"Typical 90" (Typical90):** This is a legendary contest series in the Japanese competitive programming community. It is specifically designed to cover 90 "typical" patterns you will encounter in high-level contests. The selection above (approx. 50 problems) covers the most frequent "must-know" patterns.
3. **Doubling (Binary Lifting):** Pay special attention to **Problem 058** in Typical 90. AtCoder loves "Doubling" (used to simulate $2^k$ operations in $O(\log K)$ time), which is a unique pattern that often appears in their harder problems.

### Tips for AtCoder Mastery

* **The "AtCoder Problems" App:** Use [kenkoooo.com/atcoder](https://kenkoooo.com/atcoder/) to track your progress. It sorts problems by difficulty, which is essential because AtCoder’s ABC (AtCoder Beginner Contest) "C" and "D" problems are often harder than typical "Easy/Medium" problems on other platforms.
* **Focus on the "AtCoder Brain":** AtCoder problems often have a "trick" (e.g., parity, modular arithmetic, or a clever way to rephrase the problem). If you are stuck for 30 minutes, **read the editorial**. Their editorials are arguably the best in the industry for learning "how to think" about a problem.
* **Mathematical Insight:** Unlike HackerRank or Codeforces, AtCoder often tests your ability to simplify a math expression *before* you code. If your code is running slow, it usually means your mathematical model of the problem needs refinement.
