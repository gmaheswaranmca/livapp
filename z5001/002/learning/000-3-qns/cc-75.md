### Strategy for CodeChef Mastery

1. **Use the "Practice" Filters:** CodeChef’s practice section allows you to filter by difficulty (e.g., 500–1000, 1000–1200). Aim to clear the **1000–1400** range first, as this corresponds to the "Beginner/Intermediate" transition.
2. **Focus on Editorial Analysis:** CodeChef editorials are highly detailed. If you fail a problem, look for:
* **Complexity Analysis:** Why did your $O(n^2)$ approach get a TLE (Time Limit Exceeded) while the editorial's $O(n \log n)$ passed?
* **Edge Cases:** Check if your code handles cases where the input is $0$, negative, or at the maximum constraint limit.


3. **Contest Participation:** Register for **Starters** (CodeChef’s regular rated contests). They provide a live environment that trains you to handle "easy" problems quickly, leaving more time for the harder ones.
4. **Integrated Learning:** Use the [CodeChef Learn Roadmap](https://www.codechef.com/roadmap/data-structures-and-algorithms) alongside your practice. It maps specific theory (like "Graph Traversal") to actual practice problems.

### Tips for Efficiency

* **The 20-Minute Rule:** If you are staring at a screen for more than 20 minutes without a clear direction, read the *first* hint provided by the platform.
* **Version Control for Solutions:** Maintain a local folder of your solved codes. Reviewing your own logic a week later is one of the best ways to solidify your understanding of a specific pattern.
* **Time Management:** As you advance, practice setting a timer for each problem. Easy problems should take no more than 15 minutes; medium problems should fall within 30–45 minutes.
